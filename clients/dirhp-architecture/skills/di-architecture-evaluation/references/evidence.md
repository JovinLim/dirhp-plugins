# Evidence for architectural decisions

Keep the report proportional to the task. For a multi-criterion review, use:

| Criterion and source | State and scope | Basis | Target | Result | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Designer's opening width | Snapshot or staging; selected hosts | Faces, frame, units, fidelity | Supplied minimum | Reading or refusal | Meets / does not meet / unresolved |

Preserve stable handles for a later drill-down. Report counts of evaluated and unresolved candidates
when the set is large. A sample does not establish a verdict for the full set; retain paging and
truncation information.

An example distinction:

- A successful centreline spacing reading describes the reference layout.
- A successful face-to-face measurement describes the clear gap for those operands.
- A missing mesh leaves the physical clearance unresolved.

Do not turn the first result into the second or the third into a failed criterion. If the requested
threshold is close to the uncertainty of the method, say that the evidence cannot resolve it yet.

For area schedules, state whether shared boundaries, voids, projections, and repeated occurrences
are included. A footprint area can support massing comparison under an agreed convention; it does
not automatically equal a statutory gross floor area.
